Preterm birth remains a leading cause of infant illness and death, and in most cases we still cannot predict or prevent it. The cervix has to stay firm and closed for most of pregnancy, then soften and open at the right time. That transformation is written in the structure of its collagen network.

We characterize cervical and uterine tissue across scales: second harmonic generation (SHG) microscopy to map collagen fiber organization, indentation to measure local mechanical properties, and computational models that connect the two. Through the CERES project, we study how extracellular matrix remodeling differs in pregnancies that end in spontaneous preterm birth.
